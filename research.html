Five research gaps survive 2026's memory rush

Five gaps are still open where human feedback, weighted procedure graphs, retirement, multi-tenant scale and self-changing harnesses meet, and the most open one is not where the project started: no study in these notes measures whether a procedure's accumulated weight still predicts its quality after the LLM is upgraded. The next four, in falling order of openness, are influence between tenants through shared weights, using a human edit as step-level evidence, testing a harness self-change on logged selection probabilities before shipping it, and controlling wrong retirements across a whole library under noisy reviewers. No paper found combines real human verdicts, per-edge count weights, decay, error-controlled retirement, tenants and a self-changing harness, yet almost every part was published between April and November 2026, so "we combined them" will not carry a paper. Two founding assumptions also need correcting: repeated fine-tuning loses knowledge only in some settings, and external memory forgets too. The project's own evidence is synthetic data and a simulated drive compared against a static first choice, so it shows the machinery runs and does not separate this design from standard methods. The recommended first paper is the model-upgrade measurement: it is the most open gap, needs no reviewer panel at first, and its paired-run setup feeds the later papers. Start collecting double-reviewed engineer verdicts at the same time, because that data is the slowest to gather and the hardest for others to copy. Every "not found" below means not found in the searches run for these notes up to 10 November 2026, so novelty must be rechecked just before submission.

What is filled and what is open on each axis

Throughout, a weight means a/(a+b): a counts evidence for a procedure, b counts evidence against it, and the ratio is the average of what statisticians call a Beta distribution, so "Beta weight" below means this. The prior is the estimate before any evidence, and the posterior is the estimate after the evidence has been counted. A false retirement means retiring a procedure that is still good. Fine-tuning loses knowledge only in some settings, and memory forgets too

The motivating claim is true for naive, full-parameter, sequential fine-tuning and for memorising facts. Across an 8-dataset task stream, LLaMA-2-7B-Chat's GSM8K score collapsed from 26.08 to 3.49 (TRACE). A second factoid training stage cut stage-one recall to an average of 13.5% on key-value recall (Chen et al., TMLR 2026). Weight editing is worse: ROME and MEMIT decline gradually and then collapse, with ROME's turning point between about 100 and 1,000 edits on GPT-J (Gupta et al.).

It is overstated as a general statement. The largest measurement found forgetting far smaller than the continual-learning literature suggests: about 6.7 to 10.8% for instruction tuning across about 30 model-training combinations and 12 benchmarks, and its authors warn that some of this may be a failure to elicit knowledge rather than knowledge loss (Harmon et al.). Part of the measured loss is suppression rather than erasure (Kotha et al., ICLR 2024; Zheng et al., ICLR 2025, abstract only). Continual pre-training on about 1B tokens left knowledge nearly unchanged (ARC 53.6 to 52.0) while alignment degraded (Li and Lee). Mitigations also work: LoRA (training small add-on adapters instead of the whole model) cut forgetting from 19.9% to 0.6% on a BERT-scale study, at the cost of final accuracy falling from 64.5% to 59.5% (Pandey), and routed per-task adapters reach 0.01 forgetting (Latent-LoRA). Those near-zero figures come from isolating each task and routing to it, which is the same routing problem external memory has.

External memory has its own forgetting. In the only controlled long-stream head-to-head found, WikiBigEdit, retrieval beat editing and LoRA fine-tuning but still decayed on older timesteps, and only about 10% of multi-hop samples retrieved both facts (Thede et al., ICML 2025). With a frozen model, the design with the best forward transfer (how much old experience helps new tasks, +15.0 on BabyAI) had the worst backward transfer (how much new learning damages old tasks, −10.0), with two runs per condition (Hu et al.). Continuous LLM rewriting of memory took ARC-AGI accuracy from 100% to 52.6% by round 10 (Zhang et al.). Deprecated records also persist: under direct insertion, revoked records were returned in all 81 scenario-model pairs on both exposed systems (Shen, Toyoda and Leung). No head-to-head for procedures was found, so "no fine-tuning means no forgetting" does not follow from any published result. The defensible claim is narrower: explicit weights on an external graph are auditable, reversible and cheap to update, and any claim about retention has to be measured. One single-author preprint with a dramatic 94.2% headline should not be cited, because the figure does not appear in its body (arXiv 2601.18699).

The field turned from building to auditing between April and November 2026

Volume alone explains the risk. Abstracts containing the exact phrase "agent memory" rose from 33 to 340 for the same April-to-November window a year apart (arXiv API counts). Title matches for skill or procedural work went from 4 at ICLR 2026 to 75 at EMNLP 2026, a rough signal because the venues differ. The thin categories stayed thin: 4 abstracts matched retirement with agent or procedural memory, and 3 matched human feedback with memory, though phrase counts undercount. One harness query alone returned 15 to 27 new papers per week through October and early November, some of them off-topic.

The direction of travel has seven parts, in order of how strongly the record supports them. Questions moved from "does it improve?" to "is the improvement real?". Acceptance moved from comparing scores to statistical gates. Retirement became a first-class topic, going from heuristics to analysed statistics in about five months. Continual learning arrived at harness level. Joint evolution of weights and harness began moving away from the frozen-model setting, which is a direct threat to any "never train the LLM" framing. The first production reports appeared. Human input stayed peripheral: in harness work people act as a release gate, not as the learning signal. In attack research, the focus shifted from poisoning the store to poisoning the step that promotes experience into a skill. Self-changing harnesses often overfit, by 54% of the gain in one audit

A self-changing harness is exactly what the proposed extension would be, so this evidence matters most. Across 21 of 25 settings with significant search-set gains, overfitting averaged 54% of the gain, and on ALFWorld 89%; some evolved harnesses made zero LLM calls, and four settings fell below baseline on test (Harness-Delta Attribution). In HarnessDev, feedback-set and held-out scores (held-out means tasks not used during tuning) moved in the same direction in only 34 of 64 version switches, 8 of the 64 regressed on both benchmarks, and only 2 of 9 declared versions were best on held-out tasks (HarnessDev). Under matched budgets on Terminal-Bench 2.1, harness evolution averaged 67.4 passes on first try against 72.3 for simple parallel sampling, and it changed held-out results by +1.2, 0.0 and −1.4 across three models (Wang et al.). With the model fixed, swapping harness flipped as many tasks as rerunning the same harness, 13% each (Liu and Han, abstract only).

Accumulation is a separate failure. On one benchmark, paired gain fell from +0.017 in the first quartile to −0.016 in the last as active rules grew from 13 to 70, each one individually admitted (Self-Healing Harness). That result means per-edge weights cannot catch cumulative drift by themselves. The most direct warning about putting harness elements under Beta weights is AEL, where every optional addition lowered Sharpe ratio (return per unit of risk): planner evolution −1.72, per-tool selection −1.70, cold-start priors −1.31 and skill extraction −1.11, with the authors blaming data starvation and noisy credit (AEL). Human-reviewed work is likely sparser still. Self-grading is unsafe: across 35 model-game cells every run ended at a self-score of at least 0.70 while 15 final policies scored below random (Guo et al.).

The premise is not false, only conditional. An evolved harness did transfer to SWE-bench-Verified at 75.6% against a 75.2% seed and to other models at +2.3 to +10.1 points (AHE), and gains compounded only when regression control was inside the search loop (Wang, Kattakinda and Feizi). Lilian Weng's summary is the right design rule: the evaluator and permission control "should likely sit outside the loop that evolves harness" (Weng).

The four-way signal has a measured reliability problem

The four signals are correct, correct_with_edits, wrong and wrong_with_edits. This is where the project's novelty and its measurement problem coincide. No scheme crossing right/wrong with edited/not edited was found, and no inter-rater figure (how often two people give the same verdict) for a "correct with edits" label in any engineering setting was found. One arXiv query on user edits failed to return data, so this negative is weaker than others in the report.

Partial credit is the noisiest label. In a 100-output audit, three-way agreement was 80.0% (κ = 0.699) against 93.0% (κ = 0.847) once correct and partial were merged, with disagreements "concentrated around partial credit boundaries" (A-TMA). κ (Cohen's kappa) is agreement corrected for chance, where 1 is perfect and 0 is what guessing would give. That audit compares a human with a 70B model judge, not two humans. Asking reviewers to type the correct value produced fewer corrections and more acceptance of wrong suggestions, with slightly higher overall accuracy (68% against 66%, p = .028, where p is the chance of a result this extreme if there were no real effect), and in the same 2,784-annotator design spelling errors were caught 82% of the time and errors needing rule knowledge 31% (Beck et al.). The same annotator disagrees with themselves on 19.9% of similar PRISM pairs and 35.7% of PluriHarms pairs (Ghafouri et al., ICML 2026).

Reviewers also drift. Among 400 repeat reviewers across 11,429 reviews over seven months, approval rose from 30.1% to 36.8% and inline comments fell 22% (Habituation at the Gate). Typing alone does not guarantee a gain: a typed memory was not reliably distinguishable from untyped retrieval on either benchmark (MERIT, abstract only). A half-credit signal has a numeric trap too, because it grows a+b without moving the mean; one real reward moves a fresh mean by 0.167 but after 200 neutral settlements only 0.0025, a 67-fold loss of responsiveness (SuperLocalMemory 4.0). Bias can also disable retirement entirely: with retirement margin τ, once the chance that a true failure is passed reaches (1−τ)/2 (0.45 at τ = 0.10), no procedure is retired at any sample size (The Blind Curator). And if reviewers see a procedure's score before judging, the process can fall into the case where no algorithm can make its average regret shrink over time (Tang and Ho); regret is the reward lost by not always choosing the best option. The value given to correct_with_edits therefore has to be estimated, not set by hand, and the weight has to be hidden at review time.

The current evidence shows the machinery runs, not that the design wins

The project's evidence is synthetic data and a simulated drive, and it should not be presented as real-world proof. Its simulation compares the learned choice with a static first choice across three procedures at 55, 70 and 90 percent success. That comparison is against the worst option by construction, and any standard bandit is expected to find the best arm on a stationary stream (Agrawal and Goyal), so the result does not separate this design from ordinary methods. A reviewer will ask for four comparisons on a stream where quality actually changes: stationary Thompson sampling (draw a random plausible value for each option from its Beta distribution and pick the highest), discounted Thompson sampling, sliding-window UCB (pick the highest recent estimate plus a bonus for uncertainty), and a MemRL-style value update. The reviewer will also ask how many human reviews the method needs.

Reviewer habits make this concrete. In the public ICLR 2026 review mirror, baseline complaints appeared in 43% of reviews of not-accepted memory papers against 29% of accepted ones, by a crude keyword match (ICLR 2026 mirror). A reviewer called one synthetic task "a boutique, somewhat fabricated one" (MemoryAgentBench review record), and an accepted industry paper shows synthetic training improving in-distribution retrieval while degrading real and out-of-distribution performance (When Synthetic Data Hurts). The project's real asset is unused so far: no public PCIe or NVMe trace-debugging data with human corrections was found, and none of five major 2026 accepted-paper listings has PCIe or NVMe in a title (ICML, NeurIPS). Real captures with real engineer verdicts would be unusual, but only if they exist.

Every idea below needs the same floor: at least two recognised benchmarks plus any bespoke one, a full-history baseline, a budget-matched plain agent, an ablation (switch one part off and measure the loss) for each signal and for decay and retirement, cost and latency, several independent repeat runs with their variance, a split by time so later corrections cannot leak backward, and the design frozen before private data is read. Each idea then adds its own evaluation, stated in its section.

The five ideas in full

Idea 1. Does experience survive the upgrade? Measuring procedure weights across LLM versions

The gap. Content portability is crowded; evidence portability is not. The closest paper shows that what happens to stored content when a model changes depends on its form: a fixed-schema graph moved +0.0004 ± 0.0020, while model-written notes moved +9.91 points in one direction and −13.28 in the other. That paper does not study weights or trust scores and explicitly does not test procedural memory (Goyal and Ray). The exact difference that remains: they ask whether stored content still works; nobody has asked whether stored evidence about that content is still true. Beyond Gain comes nearest from the retirement side, finding that verdicts flip for 14 to 23% of skills between model pairs, but it makes a fresh per-model judgment and carries no history (Beyond Gain). Many Steps' Proposition 23(iii) bounds the chance that betting evidence carried across an unannounced change produces a wrong decision, but it does not say how much to discount (Many Steps into One).

Why it matters. In a never-retrain design the weights are the only thing the system learns, and the LLM underneath them will change. Skills are model-relative: the same skills helped one model by 31.1 points and hurt another by 8.8 across 103 task-skill pairs (Yang et al., abstract only), and each executor's own revision beat a transferred skill by 8 to 15 successes (SkillRevise). One harness fell from 69.3 to 33.0 when its executor changed, because of a hard-coded step limit (HarnessDev), and provider aliases may shift without notice (Nakayashiki). If weights do not transfer, the organisation must re-earn them from its scarcest resource, engineer reviews; if they are carried blindly, old evidence against a procedure raises the chance of retiring it wrongly after the upgrade. Either way every model upgrade is an unmanaged risk, and no published rule says what to do.

What is claimed and contributed. The claim, a hypothesis to be tested: a procedure's accumulated weight predicts its post-upgrade quality when the procedure encodes the task, and predicts it poorly when it encodes the old model's prompt conventions, so a per-class discount estimated from a small replay beats keeping everything, resetting, and one global discount. The contribution is the first measurement found of evidence portability, a discount rule, and a reusable paired-run protocol. A null result, in which no discount beats the simple ones, is still a contribution, and negative results were in scope at the NeurIPS Evaluations & Datasets track.

How to do it. Treat an upgrade as one event that multiplies evidence above the prior by a factor a0 between 0 and 1, in the spirit of the power prior from classical statistics (Ibrahim and Chen 2000, UNVERIFIED). Estimate a0 per procedure class by running a small sample of tasks on both model versions, borrowing the idea of describing a model by its errors on representative prompts (UniRoute). Use at least two real pairs of model versions, one same-family upgrade and one cross-family, on benchmarks with objective outcomes: AFTER (382 enterprise tasks), the procedural group in EvoMemBench and SkillShift-Bench, with the project's PCIe and NVMe procedures as a clearly labelled case study. Baselines are keep everything (a0 = 1), reset (a0 = 0), one global a0, no memory, similarity-only retrieval, discounted Thompson sampling, a MemRL-style value and a budget-matched plain agent. Metrics are rank correlation (1 means the two orderings agree exactly and 0 means no relation) between pre-upgrade weight and measured post-upgrade success, wrong retirements after the upgrade, Goyal and Ray's Cost-to-Recover, the human verdicts needed to return to pre-upgrade quality, and tokens and latency. Five experiments: predictiveness by procedure class and upgrade direction; a sweep of a0; per-class against global a0 at several replay budgets; re-testing retired procedures on the new model; and sensitivity to simulated reviewer noise.

New evaluation it needs. The simulated drive contains no real model change, so it cannot speak to this. Every headline number must come from real model-version pairs on held-out tasks, with confidence intervals (a range built to contain the true value in a stated share of repeated experiments), and a small real-reviewer subset must confirm that verifier outcomes stand in fairly for human verdicts.

When. Weeks 1 to 2: rerun the novelty sweep and read the items listed for this idea at the end of the report. Weeks 3 to 8: one model pair, one benchmark, first predictiveness result, then a go/no-go decision. Weeks 9 to 16: second pair, discount comparison, reviewer subset, writing. Submit to TMLR, rolling (ANNOUNCED), at about week 18, or hold for NeurIPS 2027 main or Evaluations & Datasets, about May 2027 (PROJECTION), or EMNLP 2027 via ARR, about May 2027 (PROJECTION). ACL 2027 via ARR on 4 Jan 2027 (ANNOUNCED) is possible only for a stripped single-pair version, and I would not aim for it.

Risk. Scooping risk is high because the study is cheap and Goyal and Ray have named the hole. Expect these objections: "an upgrade is just one large decay step", which is why the three simple baselines are obligatory; "verifier outcomes are not human verdicts"; and "empirical only". The result may simply be that evidence transfers uniformly or not at all, which makes a shorter paper.

Idea 2. Whose verdict counts? Shared weights as a channel between tenants

The gap. Tenant isolation today is about what can be read. A weight is a derived value, so it can carry influence across a boundary even when all content is partitioned. Pooled same-team retrieval leaks another user's memories in 70 to 100% of cases, with 10 samples per condition and no feedback or weights in the study (MemLeak). One paper warns that "a rule supported by most clients may overwrite correct knowledge held by a minority client", but it considers no malicious clients and no promotion over time (FedWorld). Promotion and demotion across levels exists, on synthetic feedback with no adversary or isolation (HiPS). The exact difference that remains: no study found lets one tenant's verdicts change another tenant's ranking through a shared number and measures the harm or bounds it. Eight separate searches also found no attack on the numeric reinforcement weights of agent memory through a human feedback signal.

Why it matters. Pooling is how a deployment gets enough evidence, since fine-grained arms starve on their own (AEL), yet a pooled a/(a+b) has FedWorld's overwrite problem by construction. Moving it is cheap: k injected positive signals shift the mean by k(1−mean)/(a+b+k), so at a+b = 12 and mean 0.5, three flips move it by 0.1. Recurrence is decisive in the nearest attack: one recurrence gave 5 of 25 successes, two gave 21 of 25, three gave 25 of 25 (PoisonedEvolution), and a rule-priority attack persisted after 50 benign queries (OEP). In a validation lab the concrete case is one customer's drive quirks retiring a procedure that another customer's drives still need, with nobody having read anybody else's data.

What is claimed and contributed. The claim: read-isolated shared memory still transmits influence through weights, and partial pooling with a per-reviewer influence budget limits that influence while keeping most of the benefit of pooling. The contribution is a definition and measure of cross-tenant weight influence, a threat model for verdict channels, a promotion rule based on across-tenant variance in place of fixed thresholds, and a stated bound on how far one tenant's verdicts can move another tenant's estimate.

How to do it. Use a three-level hierarchy (agent, tenant, global) in which each tenant's Beta prior comes from a shared prior, so a shared procedure is simply one with low variance across tenants. The theory exists for Gaussian (bell-curve) rewards (HierTS, empirical Bayes bandits); no version for count weights on procedures was found. Data: AFTER roles as tenants, since its cross-role transfer turns gains into losses of 4.8 to 7.5 points; AIDev repositories as tenants with real human review signals, stated as a proxy; and the project's system with synthetic tenants as a stress test only. Baselines are fully pooled, fully separate, FedWorld's thresholds, HiPS's 60% promotion rule, MemLeak's ownership gating, PoisonedEvolution's provenance-diversity gate (promotion needs evidence from independent contributors) and robust federated aggregation. Metrics are change in tenant B's top choice and task success when tenant A's verdicts are removed, verdict flips needed to push a target procedure across the retirement line, harm to minority tenants, verdicts needed to reach a target quality, and update latency under concurrent writes. Five experiments: influence in a fully pooled graph; benign minority harm; three attackers (careless reviewer, colluding accounts, a legitimate tenant with different hardware); defences; and update cost at up to millions of edges.

New evaluation it needs. Multi-tenant scale is a goal of the system, not something the current simulation tests. This idea needs real LLM runs across tenants, at least a proxy stream of real human reviews, and explicit attackers.

When. Weeks 1 to 4: threat model and novelty check. Weeks 5 to 14: multi-tenant setup and the pooled-against-separate measurement. Weeks 15 to 24: attacks, defences and the bound. Target NeurIPS 2027, about May 2027 (PROJECTION), with AAAI-28, about July 2027 (PROJECTION), and ICLR 2028, about September 2027 (PROJECTION), as later routes; TMLR, rolling (ANNOUNCED), is always open. Security-venue dates were not in the notes.

Risk. Scooping risk is moderate to high, because the security community moves fast and Federated Agent Optimization already names reward labels as a leakage channel. Reviewers may call it click attacks plus hierarchical bandits. No real multi-tenant verdict stream is available, and the bound for count weights may be routine. This gap never had a dedicated novelty attack, so its rank is the least certain of the top three.

Idea 3. The edit is the evidence: typed human verdicts on real reviewers

The gap. Real human correction exists without numbers, and numbers exist without real humans. PRELUDE, TellOnce and Meta's expert loop store text or rules with no numeric weight. The closest numeric work is MSCE, where a user's correction or rejection lowers reliability, but it has no weighted edges, no edit classes, and its feedback experiments use an LLM mentor (MSCE). SmartVector counts accepts and corrections with decay, on one synthetic set and one seed (SmartVector). EvoRAG's ten raters are the only human-sourced edge feedback found (EvoRAG). The exact difference that remains: a human, typed, edit-bearing signal mapped to numeric updates, tested against binary on real reviewers, with the edited step used as evidence about that one edge.

Why it matters. Automated verifiers may not exist in industrial work, and human judgment appears stronger where it can be measured: one round of human diagnosis beat four rounds of self-judgment by 7.0 to 10.9% (WASP-Mem, abstract only). The harder problem is blame. A single outcome over a multi-step path cannot separate its steps without variation across paths (Boolean tomography). More samples do not fix co-retrieval credit (RoMeRL). Memories always retrieved together stay indistinguishable even after 10,000 episodes (Memory Worth). The best LLM attribution finds the failing step only 14.2% of the time (Who and When, abstract only). A human edit says which step changed, and nothing found uses that fact.

What is claimed and contributed. Three claims, all to be tested: the numeric value of correct_with_edits should be learned per reviewer rather than fixed; an edit-located observation identifies the wrong step under stated conditions on the graph; and this reaches the same quality with fewer human reviews than path-level credit. The contribution is the first agreement measurement found for an edit-typed verdict in engineering work, an observation model, identifiability conditions, and a rule for carrying a posterior across an edit, using edit size as the discount. ReSkill resets evidence at each version (ReSkill), one code project copies a parent's head start (Mnemosyne.NET), and neither is analysed for human edits.

How to do it. Model each reviewer with a confusion matrix (a table of how often that reviewer gives each verdict when the truth is each state), as in Dawid and Skene and RGPO, which requires several reviewers on some of the same items. Have two or three engineers independently review the same outputs with the weight hidden; 300 double-reviewed outputs is my planning figure, three times A-TMA's audit. Use AIDev v5 (pr_timeline, pr_review_comments and pr_reviews) as a public proxy, mapping merged-unchanged, merged-after-changes, closed and rewritten-after-rejection to the four signals and stating plainly that this is a proxy. Inject wrong steps with known truth to score blame. Baselines are binary collapse, uniform path credit (AEL found it beat its alternatives), rank-decayed credit (GSEM), factorial drafts (MemTrial), LLM blame and hand-set increments (EDGE), with an ablation of each signal. Metrics are κ per class pair and engineer, step-blame accuracy, reviews needed to find an injected fault, wrong retirements, reviewer minutes per item, and approval-rate drift over time.

New evaluation it needs. Real reviewers are the whole paper. A simulated reviewer cannot disagree with another reviewer, get tired, defer to automation or drift, so the current simulation has nothing to say here.

When. Weeks 1 to 3: protocol, hidden weights in the review screen, design frozen. Weeks 4 to 20: double-reviewed collection plus the AIDev analysis. Weeks 21 to 28: analysis and writing. The only duration evidence in the notes is a weak clinical proxy, a median of 6 months for execution with the middle half of projects taking 1 to 18 (Tumin et al.). Target EMNLP 2027 Industry, about June 2027 (PROJECTION), which needs the system used outside controlled environments, or ICLR 2028, about September 2027 (PROJECTION), if the public-proxy results are strong; TMLR, rolling (ANNOUNCED), remains available.

Risk. Scooping risk is lowest of the five, because human studies are slow to copy. Rejection risk is real: the typed signal may not beat binary, a small reviewer pool will be criticised, and coactive learning already covers edits in theory. A company's confidentiality rules may limit what can be shown.

Idea 4. Test the self-change on the logs first: evaluating harness and procedure changes before they ship

The gap. Off-policy evaluation estimates how a different policy would have done, using logs from the current one. The propensity is the recorded probability that the logged choice was made. Inverse propensity scoring reweights logged outcomes by one over that probability, and a doubly robust estimator adds a model of outcomes so that it is accurate if either part is good. All of this exists, and it has reached LLMs (Bhargava et al.; Ozolcer and Bae, seven users, reconstructed propensities). Its use with logged probabilities on memory or skill retrieval was not found, and the nearest harness-side work shows the problem without solving it: swapping a model rewrites 61 to 94% of later actions, leaves only about 3 to 8% of later states valid for replay, and the paper proposes no estimator and tests only model switches (The Replay Gap). The exact difference that remains is three things: procedures created by a human edit, which have no logged history; retired procedures, whose probability is zero, which breaks positivity (the requirement that every option has a non-zero chance of being chosen, Sachdeva et al.); and a harness change's effect on later weights, which per-turn estimators do not capture.

Why it matters. The overfitting evidence above means a self-change cannot be trusted on its tuning tasks. Live A/B tests take weeks (REAP, abstract only). A real harness change degraded a shipped product, and internal evaluations did not initially reproduce it (Anthropic postmortem). Of 383 replay-decided retirements in one system, 211 had improved their triggering failure but broken a protected case (Self-Healing Harness). A graph that samples its next step in proportion to its compressed weight, as GraphMind does, can log those probabilities at almost no cost, and no paper found uses them. Retirement also destroys the ability to ask whether retiring was right, unless a small re-test rate is kept.

What is claimed and contributed. The claim: a procedure graph that samples with a small exploration floor produces logs from which a per-decision estimator predicts the sign of a harness or procedure change up to a stated horizon, at a fraction of the cost of running it. The contribution is the estimator with three fixes (edited procedures inherit discounted evidence from their parent, retired procedures keep a small re-test rate, short branched rollouts estimate the effect on later weights), a map of where it fails, and the measured price of the exploration floor.

How to do it. Add an exploration floor and log probabilities, apply self-normalised inverse propensity scoring (which divides by the total weight to reduce swings) and doubly robust estimators, and report anytime-valid intervals. Treat harness elements as extra nodes so the experiment also tests whether AEL's data starvation appears under human-rate labels. Use Terminal-Bench 2.1 and SWE-bench Verified with HarnessDev and EvoHarnessBench. Ground truth comes from actually running each candidate change on held-out tasks in branched rollouts. Baselines are naive log replay, Self-Harness's held-in and held-out rule, SAGE's exact paired test, ParetoSaddler's Bayesian bootstrap (a resampling estimate of uncertainty), and deterministic selection with no floor. Metrics are sign agreement and absolute error against branched truth, how often intervals contain the truth, estimator variance by horizon, task success lost to the floor, and compute saved.

New evaluation it needs. The simulated drive has no harness changes and no logged selection probabilities. This idea needs real LLM runs, a set of real candidate changes, and paired branched rollouts as ground truth.

When. Weeks 1 to 4: survey long-horizon off-policy evaluation, which the notes did not cover, and add the floor and logging. Weeks 5 to 18: candidate changes and branched rollouts. Weeks 19 to 30: estimator comparison and writing. Target ICLR 2028, about September 2027 (PROJECTION), or AAAI-28, about July 2027 (PROJECTION), if the work runs early. SIGIR 2027 on 14 and 21 Jan 2027 (ANNOUNCED) and KDD 2027 Cycle 2 in February 2027 (PROJECTION) are too soon; TMLR, rolling (ANNOUNCED), remains available.

Risk. The expected objection is "inverse propensity scoring plus doubly robust, cite Dudík; anytime-valid, cite Waudby-Smith", and logging propensities is engineering habit, not a contribution. Randomising selection has a cost that must be justified, and long horizons may defeat the estimator, as The Replay Gap warns. Scooping risk is high because statistical gating is the fastest-closing part of harness work, and Agentic Routing and Amortized OPE (abstract only) are adjacent.

Idea 5. Retire without false alarms: library-wide control under noisy human reviewers

The gap. False discovery rate (FDR) is the expected share of flagged items that were flagged wrongly. An e-value is a number that behaves like a bet's payoff: if the hypothesis is true it averages at most 1, so a large value is evidence against it. An e-process is a running sequence of these that stays valid whenever you stop, and e-BH is a selection rule that controls FDR using e-values. Applying e-BH to per-procedure e-processes already controls library-wide FDR at any stopping time, under any exploration rule and any dependence, as a direct corollary of a 2021 result, but that result treats observations as true (Xu, Wang and Ramdas). Many Steps covers one memory per template with an exact verifier and lists noisy verifiers as future work (Many Steps into One). Blind Curator bounds failure to retire, not false retirement (The Blind Curator). The possible closest paper could not be read: Zhou and Liu, AAAI 2026, UNVERIFIED, described in search snippets as covering corruption, anytime FDR and multi-agent settings (AAAI landing page, blocked). The exact difference that remains: library-wide false-retirement control when reviewer error is unknown and one human reviews each use, a rate-type statement for the decayed and capped rule, and reversal. A lifetime guarantee is impossible for a decayed, capped rule, because bounded memory means a long enough bad run will eventually cross any fixed line (Wu makes the discounted case, unverified independently).

Why it matters. Wrong retirement has measured costs. A harsh rule took gain to −0.019, below having no skills at all (Library Drift). A common rule removed 57.5% of skills the model could not do itself (Beyond Gain). 29.5% of EDGE's pruned edges had succeeded at least once (EDGE). Selection makes it worse in a known direction: sampled less, low-weight procedures have estimates biased downward (Nie et al.), and the error is not corrected because the item stops being tried (Denrell and March). A false-deprecation rate on a graph did not appear in anything read, and no study measures the delay or quality of reinstatement.

What is claimed and contributed. Narrowed claim: separate the two jobs. A decayed weight ranks; an undecayed e-process with a small audit stream decides retirement. Then the share of wrong retirements across the library stays below a target whatever the reviewers' quality, while retirement can still fire below the Blind Curator limit. For the decayed, capped rule itself, report the mean uses before a false retirement, computed as a finite Markov chain (a process whose next state depends only on its current state). Do not claim error control for "a rule that decays toward a prior" unless it is proved. The contribution is a deployable recipe, a rate-type analysis, and a cost metric not found in these papers: time spent wrongly retired.

How to do it. Build per-procedure e-processes against "true quality is at or above the retirement level", and select with e-BH. Add a gold audit at known probabilities through prediction-powered e-values, which stay valid however wrong the cheap signal is. With one reviewer per use, an unknown error rate cannot be identified from the verdicts alone, so an audit or double-review requirement must be stated. Treat reinstatement as a new hypothesis with its own budget. Estimate the reviewer false-pass rate with a defect-injection audit. Choose one decay clock and justify it, because per-step, per-pull and wall-clock decay have different theory, and a cap and per-update decay are largely redundant. Latch deprecation, or decay toward the prior will lift a retired item back up, as Cairn shows with re-entering bad agents. Use injected wrong and injected healthy procedures with known truth on public skill sets plus a human panel; synthetic runs are legitimate for checking the guarantee, not for showing usefulness. Baselines are fixed thresholds (Ratchet, Bayesian-Agent), Many Steps' CUSUM, EvoSteer's sign test, no retirement, and random retirement of the same size. Metrics are FDR on retirements, mean uses to false retirement, detection delay, statistical power (the share of truly bad procedures caught), time wrongly retired, audit labels needed, and downstream task success.

New evaluation it needs. The current simulation has no reviewer noise model tested against truth and no library-wide error measure. This idea needs injected truth, real reviewer error, and a check that retirement still fires.

When. Weeks 1 to 3: read Zhou and Liu and the other leads, and re-scope or stop if they cover this. Weeks 4 to 12: theory, with a statistician as co-author. Weeks 13 to 24: experiments. Target NeurIPS 2027, about May 2027 (PROJECTION), with ICLR 2028, about September 2027 (PROJECTION), as fallback; TMLR, rolling (ANNOUNCED), remains available. ICML 2027 at about 22 Jan 2027 (PROJECTION) is realistic only for a theory-only version.

Risk. Scooping risk is the highest of the five: two independent groups have worked here since May 2026 and Many Steps names noisy verifiers as next work. Expect "this is just e-BH", "one reviewer per item cannot identify reviewer error", "a human verdict is not ground truth", and "gold audit plus prediction-powered e-values solves it if gold exists". Many Steps also reports that correcting for known noise was indistinguishable from not correcting, so a theory contribution may not show up in outcomes. Many Steps' theorems have not had independent peer review, and its authors disclose that AI assistants drafted proofs, code and text (Many Steps into One).

First paper: measure whether experience survives the upgrade

Start with idea 1, and start idea 3's data collection in parallel. Idea 1 is the most open gap and the cheapest to test. It runs on compute and public benchmarks, so it does not depend on the project's synthetic data or a reviewer panel at first, and its paired runs and injected-truth tasks are reused by ideas 4 and 5. Idea 3's double-reviewed verdicts are slow to gather and form the one asset competitors cannot produce quickly. Working title: "Does Experience Expire? Measuring Whether Procedure Weights Survive LLM Upgrades."

The go/no-go point is week 8. Stop or re-scope if the novelty sweep finds a direct hit, or if weights neither predict quality on the old model nor separate procedures on the new one, since that would mean the weights carry too little signal to study. If rank correlation is high and uniform across classes, the paper becomes a short "keep everything" result for TMLR.

Five design rules cost little and protect every later paper. Hide the weight at review time. Keep reviewer identifiers. Filter deprecated procedures at retrieval, because a mark without enforcement is inert (Shen, Toyoda and Leung). Log selection probabilities and keep a small exploration floor. Assert that weights actually move and report the share of uses that reached a settled signal, because one published system's posteriors never left their priors across 459 arms and 5,657 plays (SuperLocalMemory 4.0).

Pitch for the manager

Over the last seven months researchers have rushed into agent memory; abstracts on the topic rose about tenfold year on year. Our design, where engineers' verdicts change weights on a graph of procedures and the LLM is never retrained, sits close to many of those papers, and the formula itself is no longer new. One practical question has no published answer: when we upgrade the LLM, do the weights our engineers earned still mean anything? I propose a study of about five months that answers it on real model versions and public benchmarks. It uses compute more than engineer time. Whatever the result, we get a tested rule for every future model upgrade. In parallel, two senior engineers double-review a small stream of outputs, which gives us our first measurement of how consistent our reviewers are, and data no competitor can copy quickly. Today our numbers come from synthetic data and a simulated drive, so the study's first job is to replace them with real evidence. If another group publishes first or reviewers pass on it, the tools and the measurements stay with us.

Conclusion

The open space has moved away from how a weight is computed and toward when a weight can be believed: after a model change, across a tenant boundary, from a tired reviewer, on a step nobody isolated, or at the moment it retires something. That is good news for an industrial team, because those questions reward access to real engineers and real captures more than they reward another memory architecture.

The audit wave changes what a publishable contribution looks like. A system claiming a few points of gain now enters the most contested space, and such gains often shrink under fair budgets, so a careful measurement that kills a common assumption is often the stronger submission. The project should aim to be the group that knows what its weights are worth, and be ready to report the answer even when it is unflattering.
